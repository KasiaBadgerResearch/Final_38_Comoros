# How have infant, under-five, and neonatal deaths evolved relative to one another in Comoros between 1980 and 2020?

## Abstract

Using World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI), this study examines the evolution of infant, under-five, and neonatal mortality in Comoros between 1980 and 2020. The analysis compares three key indicators: number of infant deaths, number of under-five deaths, and number of neonatal deaths. Over this forty-year period, all three mortality indicators declined substantially and consistently, reflecting major improvements in child health outcomes. The reduction in deaths was particularly steep during the 1990s and early 2000s, coinciding with broader global efforts to improve maternal and child health. Although neonatal mortality declined more slowly relative to infant and under-five mortality, all categories showed significant progress by 2020. These patterns reflect sustained public health interventions, improved access to healthcare services, and progress toward global development goals focused on child survival.

## 1. Question

How have infant, under-five, and neonatal deaths evolved relative to one another in Comoros between 1980 and 2020?

- **Infant deaths proxy**: Number of infant deaths
- **Under-five deaths proxy**: Number of under-five deaths
- **Neonatal deaths proxy**: Number of neonatal deaths

## 2. Data

- **Source**: World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI)
- **Indicators**:
  - Number of infant deaths
  - Number of under-five deaths
  - Number of neonatal deaths
- **Coverage**: Comoros, 1980–2020
- **Notes**: National-level data only

## 3. Method

1. Filtered dataset for Comoros and selected the three child mortality indicators.
2. **Extracted relevant columns**: Year, Indicator Name, and Value.
3. Pivoted the dataset to create a chronological comparison of infant, under-five, and neonatal deaths between 1980 and 2020.
4. Produced a multi-line graph showing the relative decline across all three mortality categories over time.

(Analysis is descriptive; no causal inference applied.)

## 4. Results

- **Infant deaths**: Declined dramatically from the early 1980s to 2020, showing a sustained reduction in child mortality.
- **Under-five deaths**: Showed the steepest overall decline, reflecting broad improvements in early childhood survival and disease prevention.
- **Neonatal deaths**: Also decreased over the period, but at a slightly slower pace compared to other age categories, indicating persistent challenges in the earliest stages of life.
- **Comparison**: The three indicators followed nearly parallel downward trajectories, with under-five deaths consistently the highest and neonatal deaths the lowest, yet all converging toward significantly reduced levels by 2020.

## 5. Interpretation

- The steady decline across all mortality indicators reflects major improvements in maternal and child healthcare, immunization coverage, and nutrition programs implemented since the 1980s.
- The sharper reduction in under-five and infant deaths highlights effective responses to preventable diseases and expanded access to community-based healthcare.
- The relatively slower decline in neonatal deaths suggests that birth-related complications and early-life infections remain significant challenges that require targeted interventions.
- Overall, these trends demonstrate Comoros’s progress toward Sustainable Development Goal 3.2, which aims to end preventable deaths of newborns and children under five.

## 6. Limitations

- National-level data may mask regional disparities, especially between urban centers and remote islands.
- Modeled WDI estimates are subject to uncertainty, particularly for earlier years with limited data coverage.
- The descriptive analysis does not identify specific policy impacts, funding sources, or healthcare system reforms responsible for the observed decline.

## 7. Next Steps / Extensions

- Disaggregate mortality data by region, gender, and socioeconomic status to identify inequality in child health outcomes.
- Analyze links between health expenditure, immunization rates, and mortality reduction.
- Compare Comoros’s progress with that of other small island developing states (SIDS) to contextualize relative achievements and remaining challenges.
- Conduct a time-series regression to assess whether the decline in neonatal deaths has lagged significantly behind other categories, and what factors may explain the difference.
